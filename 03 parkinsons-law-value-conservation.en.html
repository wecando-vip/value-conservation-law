THE ANSWER FIRST · IN ONE SENTENCE

Parkinson's Law is, at its core, the failure of time value T: when time has no boundaries, people use "filling the hours" to stand in for "doing things well," the per-unit output E is diluted, and the total value Y quietly shrinks — the busier we look, the emptier we are.

There is only one cure — give time "multiplicative boundaries": tighten T with deadlines, calibrate E with delivery standards. The moment time returns from filler to yardstick, Parkinson's Law loses its power.

THE MULTIPLICATION LAW · THE RIGHT USE OF TIME
Y = E × S × T

E Per-unit output · S Delivery standards (acceptance · value) · T Time boundaries (deadlines · focus)

When all three exceed 1, value multiplies; only when T is bounded does E become real — time is a ruler, not a container.

THE DIVISION LAW · WHERE PARKINSON COMES FROM
E = Y / (S × T)

When delivery standards (S) are absent and time has no boundaries, work instinctively sprawls across all available time — the denominator swells, and output is diluted.

01

What Is Parkinson's Law

"Work expands so as to fill the time available for its completion."
工作会自动膨胀，直至填满所有可用的时间

Origin: In 1958, the British historian Cyril Northcote Parkinson set out the law in his book Parkinson's Law. He observed that the British Admiralty's staff grew year after year even as the fleet shrank — the more people, the more clutter, the lower the efficiency.

It is not about "laziness" — it is a law of systems: when a task has no clear standard of completion and no time constraint, it will be polished endlessly within whatever time is available. An old lady takes a whole day to post a postcard; a busy businessman posts it in three minutes. The difference lies in whether time has boundaries and delivery has standards.

FORMATION · TIME FAILS

Parkinson's Law is not "people growing lazier"; it is the denominator swelling without limit. T (time) is by nature a scarce resource — once relaxed into an "inexhaustible container," and with S (delivery standards) absent, people substitute "hours invested" for "value produced": meetings run longer, reports grow thicker. In Y = E × S × T, T bloats, E loses water, S is missing — busy on the surface, yet total value stays still.

03

Parkinson in Daily Life: We've All Been There

EXAMS

Problems solvable in an hour, given three, invite endless "double-checking" — more time, more agonizing.

WORK

Eight hours of work stretches to fill eight hours, when three would do — the rest is spent "looking busy."

MEETINGS

A meeting without an agenda or a time cap can run from afternoon to night — the content unchanged, the duration doubled.

DEADLINES

Two weeks to the deadline, two weeks of dawdling; the final two hours, done in two — efficiency is forced out.

05

How to Break It: Three Paths of the Multiplication Formula

MULTIPLY
THE CURE

Bound the Time, Set the Standard, Count the Output — Bring Time Back to the Ruler

You cannot change human nature, but you can change the design: T bounded, S defined, E measured.

REPAIR T · BOUND THE TIME
  • Hard deadlines: set the delivery time before discussing content — work without a deadline never ends
  • Time boxes: 30-minute meetings, 25-minute pomodoros — stop when the time is up
  • Self-imposed limits: cap a report at 10 pages, a proposal at 1 day
REPAIR S · ESTABLISH THE STANDARD
  • Deliverables: define what "done" means — verifiable, measurable
  • Acceptance checklists: tick every item before calling it complete (the Checklist Manifesto approach)
  • Value, not hours: evaluate results, not overtime and hours logged
REPAIR E · MAKE OUTPUT COUNTABLE
  • First things first: do the leveraged work first, and time naturally fills with "the right busy"
  • Get it right once: think before starting, and avoid "dawdling rework"
  • Review and iterate: each week, ask — "Am I filling time, or filling value?"
IN ONE SENTENCE

Parkinson's Law is not the lazy person's excuse; it is the mirror of the system — it reflects how much boundary we gave to time and how much standard we gave to delivery. Once time is bounded, value begins to multiply.

07

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does Parkinson's Law mean people are born procrastinators?

A: No. It describes a law of systems: without time boundaries and delivery standards, any work will sprawl across the time available. Change the design — it beats changing human nature.

Q: Does giving more time really produce better results?

A: Usually not. The more time, the easier it is to fall into endless polishing and agonizing. A reasonable deadline, by contrast, sharpens focus (the "deadline effect" in Scarcity).

Q: How can ordinary people use this formula?

A: Give every task three numbers — the deadline (T), the delivery standard (S), and the estimated hours (E). With all three in place, Parkinson's Law has nowhere to live.
