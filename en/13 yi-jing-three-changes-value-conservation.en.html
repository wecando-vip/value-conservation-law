ANSWER FIRST · ONE SENTENCE

The three changes of the I Ching are the philosophical prototype of the Law of Value Conservation: Simplicity — the great Way is simple; one formula, Y = E × S × T, sees through rise and fall. Change — the three values shift with the times; one must "advance in step with the hour," and multiplication and division switch as the moment turns. Constancy — beneath all change there is a law that does not change: only when the three values exceed 1 together is growth real.

The remedy lies in a new connotation of money: just as the Luoshu gathers the numbers of all things into its nine-palace grid, let currency carry economic value, social value, and time value at once, fusing the scattered three ledgers into one. When the three values are measured on one unified yardstick, yin and yang are balanced, change advances with the times, and constancy holds amid change — the self-regulating virtuous cycle begins, and nation, enterprise, and individual all win.

What the Three Changes Are

"The Changes take heaven and earth as their standard, and therefore can embrace the Way of heaven and earth. One yin, one yang — that is the Way. When the course runs out, change; when it changes, it becomes unobstructed; when unobstructed, it endures."
— The Great Treatise of the Zhou Changes

Origin: The Zhou Changes took shape around the Western Zhou, using yin and yang lines to unfold the eight trigrams and sixty-four hexagrams — one of the wellsprings of Eastern thought. The "three changes" — Simplicity, Change, Constancy — are its three levels for summarizing the changes of the cosmos: Simplicity — the complex world reduced to the simplest signs and laws; Change — all things change with the hour and must follow the moment; Constancy — within change there is a law that does not change.

The Hetu, Luoshu and numerology: the Hetu and Luoshu model the world through "images and numbers" — the Luoshu nine-palace grid, "nine at the head, one at the foot, three on the left, seven on the right" — an ancient numerological wisdom that places all things into structure and measurement, working in the same spirit as a modern "unified measurement system."

How Imbalance Forms

E

The Arrogant Dragon Repents · Yang out of Balance

Source of Division

Formation: pursuing growth and expansion alone, driving E to its summit — the top line of the Qian hexagram, "the arrogant dragon will have cause to repent": fly too high, and regret follows. Overleveraged enterprises and health-draining individuals are both "yang" that does not know where to stop.

S

Solitary Yin Does Not Grow · Trust Turns Cold

The Sacrificed Term

Formation: speaking only of interest, never of affection; keeping only accounts, never people — the Kun hexagram's "bearing all things by virtue of the earth" is set aside (S harmed); solitary yin does not grow, solitary yang does not live — lose trust and the people's hearts, and no strength of E has anything left to carry.

T

Walking Against the Hour · Missing the Moment

The Hidden Cost

Formation: the moment has changed while the old way stays — acting against the tide: the very word "change and unobstructed" is forgotten (T fails); the sun at noon declines, the moon at full wanes, and a system that does not advance with the times will be settled by the times.

Four Levels Applied

INDIVIDUAL
Yin-Yang and the Moment

Work and Rest Out of Balance: One's Personal "Arrogant Dragon"

Working without rest, advancing without retreating — yang that knows no limit breaks health and mind first.

FORMATION · IMBALANCE

The six lines of Qian, from "the hidden dragon must not act" to "the arrogant dragon repents," teach that in the course of ascent one must know when to advance and when to hold, and hold one's time and place — chasing only the yang of f(m) while draining the yin of f(h), one falls at the point of "arrogance."

REMEDY · THE MIDDLE WAY

The individual's "three changes": Simplicity — keep to three essentials; Change — adjust the rhythm to the moment; Constancy — hold the ground of health and integrity — yin and yang in mutual support, advance and retreat with measure.

FAMILY
Bearing All Things by Virtue of the Earth

Only Arguments, No Warmth: The Home Loses Its Kun

Contending over right and wrong, weighing gains and losses — forgetting Kun's "bearing all things," and affection has nothing to rest upon.

FORMATION · IMBALANCE

A family that weighs "feeling" too lightly and "reason" too heavily turns cold; weigh "reason" too lightly and "feeling" too heavily, and it turns chaotic — in a home of unbalanced yin and yang, the more rules, the more distance (S turns cold); keep the family on E's ledger alone, and affection becomes a cost that may be written off.

REMEDY · THE MIDDLE WAY

The way of the home lies in Kun: carry people with tolerance, carry affairs with trust (S's depth of virtue), with a measure of "Change" for elasticity — today's tolerance is the root that lets a family weather tomorrow's storms.

ENTERPRISE
Change and Constancy

Forgetting Change in Good Times, Forgetting the Way in Bad: Both Trap the Firm

In good times only expansion, no reserve; in bad times only cuts, no innovation — Change and Constancy, both missed.

FORMATION · IMBALANCE

In rapid expansion a firm forgets "Change" (the environment has shifted, the model has not), and when the wind turns, inventory and leverage press together (E turns against it); in a trough it cuts only budgets (S cools) and leaves culture untouched — treating "Constancy" as a slogan and "Change" as a luxury.

REMEDY · THE MIDDLE WAY

The enterprise's "three changes": Simplicity — organization and process made lean; Change — strategy adjusted to the current; Constancy — values and bottom lines held firm — in good times cultivate the power of "change and unobstructed," in bad times hold the way of "Constancy."

NATION
Change and the Art of Governance

When Institutions "Run Out," They Must Change — or the Way Is Blocked

Dynastic rise and fall turns on a single word: change — unobstructed, it endures; blocked, it perishes.

FORMATION · IMBALANCE

Successive dynasties fell at their zenith precisely because they kept only the name of "Constancy" (ancestral institutions made sacred) while refusing the substance of "Change" (institutions rigid); E's yang grew dominant while S's yin (the people's hearts) drained away, and when institutions reached the "exhausted" point they still would not change — "unobstructed, it endures" was cast aside, and the cycle of rise and fall took shape.

REMEDY · CHANGE AND OBSTRUCTION

The art of governance joins "Change" with "Constancy": Change — institutions renewed with the age; Constancy — the enduring purpose of serving the people and fairness — when the course runs out, change; when it changes, it becomes unobstructed; when unobstructed, it endures.

The Remedy: A New Connotation of Money

"The old ledger recorded only the 'yang' book; the new ledger of money, like the Hetu and Luoshu, must bring yin and yang and time into one pattern."
— The Law of Value Conservation · The Way Out

Resolving imbalance is not about abolishing change, but about modeling with Simplicity, rebalancing with Change, and holding the Way with Constancy: economic value shifts from "arrogance" to "measure" (multiplication of E); social value shifts from "sacrificed" to "cherished" — trust, human warmth and fairness all enter the ledger (multiplication of S); time value shifts from "over-drawn" to "measured" — following the age and investing in the future (multiplication of T).

The new connotation of money — let currency carry three values at once:

· Economic value E (yang): every yuan corresponds to real goods and services created, ending "printing without producing";
· Social value S (yin): currency circulates on trust — rewarding honest dealing, mutual care, and the warmth of human feeling;
· Time value T (the hour): money is a promise across generations — education, innovation, green growth and continuity are measured, encouraging us to "advance with the times."

When money measures E, S and T at once, as the Luoshu gathers all things into the nine-palace grid — yin and yang in mutual support, advancing with the times, holding constancy amid change: the division formula loses its ground, the multiplication formula begins to self-regulate, and the virtuous cycle is triggered.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What do the "three changes" of the I Ching mean?

A: Simplicity — the complex world reduced to the simplest law; Change — all things shift with the hour and must follow the current; Constancy — within change there is a law that does not change. Together they form an Eastern system of thought about "change and balance."

Q: What do the Hetu and Luoshu have to do with modern life?

A: The Hetu and Luoshu gather the numbers of all things into the nine-palace structure — in essence an ancient wisdom of "structured measurement." The modern version is the unified ledger — bringing economic, social and time value into one system of measure, so that every act of giving finds its place.

Q: What does the "new connotation of money" mean for ordinary people?

A: It lets money measure economic, social and time value at once. At the personal level this is the micro formula — do not let the chase for income drain your health, your relationships, or your long-term accumulation; keep f(h) and f(t), and your life earns compound interest.
